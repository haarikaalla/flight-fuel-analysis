When can we use a fuel estimate?

Explore a retrospective ADS-B fuel study by period, acceptance rule, aircraft and observation loss. Every number comes from the committed aggregate Phase 4 evaluation.

What is being estimated?

Fuel consumed during a completed, predefined flight interval, in kilograms. The reference label comes from differences in onboard fuel reports; the predictors use aircraft type, interval duration and ADS-B observations. With no usable ADS-B position, the estimate falls back to the training-period aircraft-type median rate.

This demo compares published group results. It does not predict fuel for a new flight, present calibrated individual uncertainty, or identify fuel-saving opportunities.

How to present this result

Finding: the zero-input fallback improved October average absolute error; contiguous loss of points damaged the ADS-B model more than scattered thinning in the controlled September sample.

Limit: the 75% learned-risk rule almost never accepts longer intervals and has very uneven aircraft access. It should not be described as a universally reliable estimator. The controlled masks are not proof about naturally missing flights.

Next evidence: independent operators, a prospective definition of interval boundaries, a cost for abstaining, and a deeper prior-work comparison. The public benchmark cannot establish those claims by itself.
